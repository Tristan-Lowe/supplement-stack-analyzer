What it covers, and what it doesn’t.

Interactions are extracted from primary sources, and every one carries the sentence it came from. A finding appears here only after it has been checked against that sentence. If a pair isn’t in the database, the checker says so — it never reports a stack as safe.

Coverage is still small. Prescription interactions come from the labels of {{DRUG_COUNT}} drugs so far: {{DRUG_LIST}}. Other medications are recognised by name — {{DRUGS_KNOWN}} in all — but only interactions written on those {{DRUG_COUNT}} labels can be found.

When a label warns about a whole class — “NSAIDs”, “antacids”, “proton pump inhibitors” — the warning reaches every drug NIH’s classification puts in that class, and the finding says so. Not handled yet: branded multivitamins by product name. Vitamin A and E doses in IU aren’t converted, because the conversion depends on the form; enter them in micrograms or milligrams. The interactions were reviewed against their sources, but not by a pharmacist. Ask one before you change anything you take.
